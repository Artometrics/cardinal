### Ch. 2 Follow the Water (¶1–12) — HOOVER / STATE POWER
- ¶4: “on a February morning in 1931, a group of twelve West Coast contractors assembled at the Engineers’ Club in San Francisco.”
- ¶6: eight firms; Kaiser named it after Chinatown’s Six Companies; $500,000 each toward the $5 million bond; “The $1.5 million contributed by Bechtel and Kaiser combined gave them a resounding 30 percent equity in the project. - ¶7: “Boulder Dam (renamed Hoover Dam in 1947)”. **Oversimplified:** Wilbur named it Hoover Dam in September 1930; Ickes renamed it Boulder in 1933; the Hoover name was restored in 1947 (Whyte p. 587; ledger 1930-09-17). - ¶8: “On November 24, 1922 … Then–US commerce secretary Herbert Hoover, longtime promoter of public-private partnerships … brokered the contentious gathering.” The Compact was signed that night in Santa Fe. **Agrees with Whyte pp. 270–271** (strengthens CANON-026). Then: “six years later, Congress took control and authorized construction of a dam.”
- ¶10: Crowe, ex-Bureau superintendent (1924), was Six Companies’ “secret weapon”; bid “$48,890,955—coincidentally just $24,000 more than government engineers had calculated”; “on March 4, 1931, the bureau awarded the contract to Six Companies.” **Date conflict:** USBR says bids were opened March 4 and the contract awarded March 11, 1931. Bid figure: McCartney p. 34 gives $48,890,000; USBR gives $48,890,995.50.
- ¶11: “At $165 million, the Boulder Canyon Project Act—signed into law by President Hoover …” **Error:** the Act was signed by Coolidge on December 21, 1928. Hoover took office in March 1929. → canon.

## External checks run for this book (November 21, 2026)
- **Boulder Canyon Project Act:** signed by President **Coolidge**, December 21, 1928 (LOC “Today in History”; National Archives milestone document: “Approved, December 21, 1928”; USBR chronology). The Hoover Presidential Library adds that the bill “was largely written by Hoover and Secretary of the Interior Hubert Work.” Effective June 25, 1929. → Denton ch. 2 ¶11 is wrong on the signer, but Hoover’s drafting role is real.
- **SRI origin:** SRI’s own corporate history (archived 2017): in **1925** “Dr. Robert E. Swain … championed the idea of an interdisciplinary research institute … Early supporters included Stanford alumnus and trustee, Herbert Hoover.” In 1927 Wilbur recommended a plan; it was tabled in 1930; in **1939** “Stanford’s faculty committee discussed the institute idea while meeting at the Bohemian Grove”; Atholl McBean was acknowledged as founder “along with the Stanford University Board of Trustees”; articles were filed **November 6, 1946**; “Stanford University trustees elected 11 SRI directors.” SRI’s 2022 heritage chapter says the charter let the trustees select SRI’s directors, and the initial directors were “32 of the most prestigious western executives.” SRI’s 75th-anniversary story says Swain pitched Hoover (“then Secretary of Commerce”) in the late 1920s, and “Hoover was enthusiastic.” → Denton’s “Initially conceived by President Hoover” overstates it: the idea was Swain’s, and Hoover was an early backer as trustee and Commerce Secretary, not President. Bechtel as a founding director rests on Gibson p. 156 (via Denton). Not yet independently seen.
- **Hoover Institution and Bechtel money (modern, verified):** Stephen D. Bechtel Jr. made “a five-year $5 million pledge for a new building at Hoover” (*Inside Philanthropy*, April 21, 2015). He was a named donor to the Traitel Building (hoover.org, March 13 and July 6, 2017). The **S. D. Bechtel, Jr. Foundation** helped fund the **George P. Shultz Building** (hoover.org, March 1, 2024). ## Cross-book synthesis (Denton vs Whyte, McCartney, canon)
1. **Denton depends heavily on McCartney** for 1898–1987 (dozens of notes cite *Friends in High Places* by page). Where they agree, Denton is not independent corroboration unless her note cites something else. Independent Denton sources: declassified Kissinger/Ford telcons (1976), State Department cables (Mindanao workers), Gibson’s SRI history, IPS/*WaPo* Aqaba documents, Bechtel’s website.
2. **SRI–Hoover–Bechtel:** the strongest Stanford “intent” material in Denton is ch. 10 ¶4–5 plus ch. 12 ¶1–2, 10. SRI is “Bechtel-dominated,” with Steve Sr. pushing international work “doubled and doubled,” Ex-Im projects “researched and selected by SRI,” and the Nixon Pacific Rim strategy “spawned at” SRI. This is a pattern of use, not a document showing Stanford decided anything for Bechtel’s benefit. It is not a smoking gun.
3. **Errors in Denton** (dates and officeholders): Boulder Act signer; March 4 vs 11, 1931; 60M tons and nine months (ch. 3); Forrestal–McCone 1950; McCone AEC 1957 and DCI “after 1962”; Shultz “colonel”; Davis “head of the AEC”; Iraq terror-list date (1982 vs November 1983); 430 vs 440 layoffs; FUSRAP origin; Shultz board list (World Bank/IMF). Cite Denton for characterization and for her unique documents, not for dates.
4. **Whyte agreement:** Denton’s November 24, 1922 Colorado River Compact brokered by Hoover agrees with Whyte.
5. **Hoover and the Grove (settled against Whyte p. 337):** Whyte quotes Hoover’s 1925 letter to Mark Sullivan: the encampment “has now become the greatest men’s party in the world.” So Denton’s ch. 10 ¶14 attribution is substantially right, though Denton has “on earth” and Whyte’s primary-letter wording is “in the world.” Whyte also shows Hoover *at the Grove* in August 1927 when the wire report of Coolidge’s “I do not choose” statement reached him. Afterwards he “remained at his cabinet post, refusing to declare a candidacy.” So Denton’s “It was at the Grove that Herbert Hoover had announced his presidential candidacy” (ch. 10 ¶17) is **wrong as stated**. The defensible version: the Grove was where Hoover and his intimates learned of Coolidge’s withdrawal and began “to spin out theories” (Whyte’s phrase is “Like so many weaving spiders,” and Denton’s ch. 10 title borrows the Grove motto).
