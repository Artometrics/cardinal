## Top findings for Cardinal
1. **San Mateo (Dec 20, 1882): Conkling's "secret journal"** (CANON-116).
   - The Southern Pacific's lawyer, a former senator and the last surviving member of the Joint Committee on Reconstruction, told the Court that the drafters chose "persons" to cover corporations. He cited the committee's unpublished journal.
   - Graham, *Everyman's Constitution*: "a deliberate, brazen forgery."
   - The Court never decided the case. About two years later Stanford's railroad paid the taxes and settled (pp. 113–143).
2. **The test-case campaign** (CANON-119).
   - The 1879 constitution taxed railroads without the mortgage deduction.
   - The SP and CP refused to pay, ran a press campaign, and brought about 63 test cases (Daily Alta, Feb. 10, 1886).
   - Team: Sanderson, Edmunds, Pomeroy, and Conkling (pp. 119–120).
3. **Field as Stanford's judge** (CANON-118).
   - Field recommended Pomeroy, then presided over the same cases on circuit.
   - He wrote "the very essence of tyranny" and leaked other justices' confidential memoranda to the railroad's lawyers.
   - Waite refused to give him a Central Pacific opinion because of his "intimate personal relations with the managers."
   - Stanford "was even said to be behind" Field's nomination (pp. 140–142).
4. **Santa Clara (May 10, 1886, 118 U.S. 394): the fence holding vs. Davis's headnote** (CANON-117).
   - Waite's note to Davis: "we avoided the constitutional question in the decision."
   - Davis printed the remark and a personhood headnote anyway; he had once been president of the Newburgh and New York Railway.
   - Field turned the headnote into precedent in Beckwith (1889): "It was so held in Santa Clara…" (pp. 144–157).
5. **Outcome framing.** Collins (1912): of 604 Fourteenth Amendment cases, 312 involved corporations and 28 involved African Americans. "Magna Charta of accumulated and organized capital" (p. 158ff.). This is already in the ep03 review.

## Do not import from Winkler
- "When Stanford established [the university] … in 1891, he asked Field to be one of its founding trustees." Field was named in the Founding Grant of Nov 11, 1885 (CANON-097, CANON-118).
- The 1861 "suitcase full of Central Pacific stock certificates" and the "$500,000 annually" in bribes. Both are unverified, with secondary cites only (CANON-120).
- "Stanford drove in the famous Golden Spike." He swung and missed at the ceremony (Chang; CANON-091).

## Ivory crossovers
- **Elihu Yale and the East India Company** (p. 26; Conclusion pp. 382–383: Yale "named after Elihu Yale, one of the stockholders made rich by the East India Company"). Matches the expansion-plan Yale formula.
- **Dartmouth College case** (ch. 3): colleges as corporations, the legal frame for a Founding Grant.
- Graham's 1938 *Yale Law Journal* article "Four Letters of Mr. Justice Field" is the source for Field's leaks to the SP.
- No Harriman, Bones or Dulles material.
