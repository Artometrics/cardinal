## Top findings for Cardinal
1. **The Bloomington visit was March 21–22, 1891, not 1890** (CANON-108).
   - Miller (pp. 53–54) says "one day in 1890," and our ledger, timeline and ep4-02 copied that.
   - McMains (*Indiana Magazine of History* 115, Sept. 2019) and Stanford Magazine date it: the Stanfords arrived Saturday, March 21, 1891, and Jordan accepted Sunday, March 22. Jordan was IU's president, and 40.
   - Fixed in the drafts and the ledger.
2. **Jane Stanford vs. Jordan, 1904: the Goebel letters** (CANON-109).
   - Jane used German professor Julius Goebel as an informant.
   - On June 6, 1904, Goebel wrote to her accusing Jordan of "whitewashing" Charles Gilbert's affair and of running the university like a "gang" (Horace Davis Papers SC0028, via Carnochan).
   - Jane wrote to the senior trustee that Jordan's moral failings had "been painfully evident to me for a long time."
   - Spoehr: by the end of 1904 "rumors abounded that Mrs. Stanford planned to replace Jordan" (pp. 69–71).
3. **Jordan in Honolulu, March 1905** (CANON-110).
   - He told the NYT (Mar. 11, 1905) the trip had "nothing whatever to do with the investigation."
   - He paid Dr. Ernest Waterhouse $350. Waterhouse never examined the body, but his memo for Jordan said he was "decidedly not" convinced of poisoning.
   - Jordan's statement offered gingerbread, exertion and angina, then "hysteria."
   - The Honolulu doctors answered: "It is imbecile to think…" Jordan attacked Dr. Humphris as "a man without professional or personal standing" and called the doctors' diagnosis a money-making conspiracy.
   - To Carl S. Smith (Mar. 24, 1905): "morally certain" (pp. 112–117).
4. **The jury's words:** strychnine "introduced into a bottle of bicarbonate of soda with felonious intent by some person or persons to this jury unknown" (p. 112, via Cutler 45). The SF *Evening Bulletin* headline of March 1, 1905, MRS. STANFORD DIES, POISONED, printed the finding ahead of the jury.
5. **Aftermath** (CANON-111):
   - After Jordan fired Goebel, the trustees stripped his power to dismiss faculty (Spoehr).
   - In 1913 he was moved to chancellor. This matches Whyte p. 124, where Hoover engineered it; Miller doesn't mention Hoover.
6. **Eugenics as Jordan's program** (CANON-112):
   - In the 1880s at Indiana he taught that "pauperism" could be "exterminated just as swamps are drained."
   - He published on eugenics from 1898: *Blood of the Nation*, *Human Harvest*, *Your Family Tree*.
   - Indiana passed its 1907 law (his "friends from Bloomington"). Miller says he "helped get" California's 1909 law passed.
   - He chaired the American Breeders Association's Eugenics Committee.
   - Miller says he persuaded **Mary Harriman** to give more than $500,000 for the Eugenics Record Office. Standard accounts credit Davenport; Jordan's memoir (vol. 2, pp. 297–98) is the basis for his role, so hedge it.
7. **Strychnine in Jordan's *Guide to the Study of Fishes* (1905), p. 430**, under "How to Secure Fishes" (CANON-113). Suggestive only; Miller presents it as a discovery, not proof.
8. **Scholarly range on the death** (useful for the Disputes tab):
   - Cutler: poisoned; Jordan covered it up.
   - Carnochan: Jordan "had the motive."
   - White: suspects Bertha Berner (for the will money); Jordan covered up; the death was "lucky" for him.
   - Spoehr: poisoned, but Jordan ordering it is "fantasy."
   - A retired Stanford archivist: Cutler is "speculation."

## Do not import from Miller
- "One day in 1890" (it was March 1891).
- "Just a year into David's presidency, Leland Stanford had died." Leland died June 21, 1893, about two years in (CANON-115).
- Jordan "persuaded" Mary Harriman, as a flat fact. Attribute it: "by his own account."
- Any suggestion that Jordan caused the death. Miller herself doesn't claim it.

## Ivory crossovers
- **Mary W. Harriman** (E. H. Harriman's widow; mother of Averell and Roland Harriman, both Yale/Skull and Bones) funded the ERO in 1910, which Jordan says he urged. A Stanford-to-Harriman link in eugenics.
- **Andrew D. White** (Cornell; Yale 1853, Skull and Bones) steered the Stanfords to Jordan (from the McMains / Stanford Magazine check, not Miller).
- Agassiz / Harvard (Penikese) as Jordan's model; Yale is not directly involved.
