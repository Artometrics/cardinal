## Top findings for Cardinal
1. **The C&F books burned** (CANON-128):
   - Before the Wilson Committee (Huntington on the stand Feb 14, 1873), Hopkins fed "fifteen sturdy leather-bound volumes" into the CP office's basement furnace. Huntington then said the records were lost: "My partner Hopkins is a peculiar man" (ch. 9 ¶10–11).
   - ep02 already matches. **Enriched** ep2-07 l.139, which had called this only Huntington's "fear."
2. **Stanford elected governor Sept 4, 1861**, nearly 56,000 votes (ch. 3 ¶33). **Rekeyed** the ledger row from the 1861-01-01 placeholder (CANON-129).
3. **Hopkins's death at Yuma, night of Mar 28, 1878** (ch. 12 ¶12). Huntington: "gone over the river… I shall not see years enough hereafter to … ever again form such a friendship" (¶13).
   - **Fixed** ep3-03, which added "to his brother" and "by wire"; neither is in Rayner.
4. **Mussel Slough** (ch. 13 ¶11–16): the weapons list, Crow "one of the best shots in California," the wild geese, Haymaker, "eight men died," the OK Corral comparison, and "more likely that all parties arrived … without clear intention to shoot." ep03 is faithful. Prices of $17–$40 an acre are confirmed (ep3-01 l.171).
5. **Nob Hill** (ch. 10 ¶6–7):
   - Stanford's mansion is confirmed (thirty-foot wall, two acres, $2 million, 50+ rooms, doors seven inches thick, 70-ft dome, $30,000 conservatory, $150 ladder), as are Hopkins's cottage and no carriage.
   - **Spite fence:** Rayner says Crocker put a forty-foot fence around the unnamed Chinese undertaker's lot. ep03's "three sides of Yung's house, so the sun barely got in" is **not in Rayner**. **Fixed** in the md (the narration copies didn't carry it).
6. **Colton Letters** came out through Ellen Colton's suit, tried Nov 1883 to Oct 1885 (ch. 14). **Fixed** the ep3-01 cite (ch. 9 → ch. 14).
7. **Cohen's attack** (ch. 10 ¶9–11): "the ambition of an Emperor and the spite of a peanut vendor." This confirms CANON-095 (CANON-131).
8. **Jane Stanford "sold her pearls"** and had no carriage, 1893–96 (ch. 17 ¶4). This supports the drafts' jewel line (CANON-132).

## Do not import from Rayner
- "Stanford and Durant took turns with silver sledgehammers, driving home the golden spike" (ch. 8 ¶29) conflicts with CANON-091 (CANON-130).
- Rayner's "Much later, in the 1880s, Stanford used his money to found the university … Today, he'd most likely have bought a sports team" (ch. 5 ¶21) is opinion.
