# The Story So Far

*Saved Nov 21, 2026. A plain-language summary of where Cardinal stands, drawn from the 8-part documentary outline, the thesis, the editor's critique and the close-reading research to date.*

The whole story follows one piece of land and one fortune for 250 years, building to the line "Rail controlled movement. Stanford controls succession."

1. **The Ledger (1769–1848).** Portolá's men camp under the lone redwood, the "tall stick." Missions turn people and land into entries in a register. It ends with gold found nine days before the border moves, and nobody at the treaty table knows.
2. **The Toll.** Four shopkeepers learn the money is in moving goods, not in the gold. Stanford swings at the last spike in 1869 and misses, while the railroad's construction-company books are kept out of sight.
3. **The Octopus.** The railroad becomes the government, the law and the race policy. Seven men die at Mussel Slough. It ends with the 1884 telegram from Florence: Leland Jr. is dead.
4. **The Boy and the Farm.** It opens on Jane Stanford's poisoning in Honolulu in 1905 and flashes back to grief turning a fortune into an institution that can't die. The farm becomes a university.
5. **The Engineer's Century.** Hoover, Stanford's first-class graduate, turns engineering into statecraft across Australia, Kaiping, Belgian relief and the White House, and then comes home to run Stanford's board. It ends in 1945 with Terman coming home with a plan for land that can't be sold.
6. **The Park.** Land that can't be sold gets leased to the future: Varian, the Industrial Park and the military research money. It ends when SRI is cut loose in 1970 and the defense work keeps going.
7. **The Road.** Kleiner Perkins opens on Stanford land at 3000 Sand Hill Road, and every company passes through that gate up to PayPal's sale in 2002.
8. **The Open Era.** January 2025: tech founders sit ahead of cabinet nominees at the inauguration, and the machine stops hiding.

## Where it's strong and where it's weak

The spine is strong, and parts 1 through 6 have real scenes and dates. The weak spots are the middle and the end. Part 5 has the factual errors the Whyte reading caught, part 7 drifts into well-known VC history, and part 8 is a mood rather than a reveal. The thesis also argues the whole thing was designed, while the evidence supports "selected, not designed." That's the gap a *Going Clear*–level series has to close.

## What the research added that the draft doesn't use yet

- **Kaiping:** the court found Hoover's side broke faith, and he tried to destroy the minutes.
- **Francqui:** a direct link between Leopold's Congo and Belgian relief.
- **Hoover's 1912 board move:** he took a seat on Stanford's board and installed his own man as president.
- **The Colorado River Compact.**

Each of these can anchor an episode: someone with power, something they did privately, and a record that survived.
